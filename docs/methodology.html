The short version

  1. Each framework casts a vote once a month: positive, neutral or negative for holding stocks.
  2. Votes are averaged inside their family first, so several similar frameworks count once.
  3. Four driver families set the direction. Two alarm families can only subtract. Valuation sets the size of any move.
  4. The score has to clear a threshold, with most families agreeing, two months in a row, before the stance changes.
  5. The stance, the Fed's direction and the inflation trend turn a neutral mix into a model allocation.

Timing and data

  • Everything is evaluated at month-end, on the last complete month. The job runs on the 2nd, so a run on December 2 reports November.
  • Market series use the month-end close. Monthly economic series are shifted by their publication delay (one month; one quarter for quarterly data), so each month only uses what had been published by then.
  • If a series is missing, the frameworks that need it show "No data" and are left out of the average. Nothing is backfilled.
  • Economic data comes from FRED. ETF prices come from Yahoo Finance (Stooq as a fallback), long-run valuation series from multpl.com (Yale as a fallback), and the sentiment survey from AAII. Outside sources can fail or block automated requests; when one does, its frameworks show "No data" and the rest carry on.
  • Series owned by third parties (index levels, the VIX, the ICE high-yield spread, ETF prices, valuation history and the AAII survey) are fine for personal use and would need a licensed source before a public launch. The sources table below marks them.

Scoring

Family score. The weighted average of its members' votes, using each framework's evidence weight (1.0, 0.75 or 0.5).

Drivers (macro regime, policy and liquidity, trend and momentum, sentiment and calendar) run from -1 to +1. The driver score is their weighted average.

Alarms (recession risk, credit and volatility) run from -1 to 0. Clear is 0, watch is -0.5, triggered is -1. An alarm that is not ringing is the absence of bad news, not good news, so it adds nothing. The alarm penalty is the average of the two.

Score = driver score + alarm penalty, limited to the range -1 to +1.

Agreement is the share of families with an opinion (a score of at least 0.25 either way) that point the same direction as the score.

Stance. Overweight needs a score of +0.5 or more and Underweight -0.5 or less, each with at least half the opinionated families agreeing. Otherwise Neutral.

Breakers. If the recession family is at -0.75 or worse, or the credit signal is triggered, the stance cannot be Overweight. This applies at once.

Confirmation. Any other change needs the same reading two months in a row and moves one step at a time.

Sizing. A step is 10 points of stocks. When the valuation family reads expensive (a score of -0.5 or lower), an Overweight is 5 points. When it reads cheap, an Underweight is 5 points.

Where to look. Multi-asset trend, sector rotation and intermarket ratios are shown for context. They are not part of the score.

Model allocation

  1. Stocks = neutral stocks plus the stance step.
  2. Alternatives (commodities and gold) = neutral plus 5 points when core inflation is higher than six months ago and at least one of gold or commodities is above its 10-month average, minus 5 when growth and inflation are both slowing.
  3. Bonds and cash share what is left: half in cash while the Fed is hiking, about a tenth once it is cutting, and the neutral split when it is on hold.

These parameters were set once, from the logic of each framework. They have not been tuned to a backtest, which is the main way combined-signal models fool their authors.

What this is not

  • It is not a forecast. It summarizes what well-known rules say about current conditions.
  • It is not personal advice. The output is the same for every visitor.
  • The frameworks named after firms are public-data versions of the same ideas, not copies of anyone's proprietary model.
  • The original macro and risk regime view is kept as the classic view, with its own methodology page.
